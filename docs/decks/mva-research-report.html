Methods & implementation · gene analysis

Variant gates define candidates, not diagnoses

AnnotateVEP/snpEff consequences and population, clinical, splice, and missense predictors.
ValidateRequire usable coordinates and alleles for each call.
FilterRare or absent population allele, plus predicted impact; a narrow unknown-frequency MVA-context exception.
CollapseGenes with at least one passing variant become Phase 1 candidate seeds.
Actual code: AF ≤ 0.001 or gnomAD not found; impact can pass via high consequence, ClinVar, CADD ≥ 20, SpliceAI ≥ 0.2, REVEL ≥ 0.5, or AlphaMissense ≥ 0.5.

Analysis & discussion

Coherent hypothesis; mixed enrichment support

Evidence that aligns

  • Primary studies establish disease-gene and chromosome-segregation mechanisms.
  • Passing-candidate genes provide a reproducible research seed set.
  • STRING reports a focused component with established checkpoint genes.

Evidence that does not align

  • Independent custom-background pathway branch has zero BH-significant terms.
  • DAVID maps fewer genes and uses its own tested universe.
  • Broad Phase 2 terms do not isolate a unique causal mechanism.
Sources: [R1–R6], [R8–R9]. Associations are not functional validation.14 / 17

Limitations · conclusion · next steps

Keep candidate status; validate before inference

Limits

  • Predicted consequences and unphased multiple hits are not proof of biallelic disease.
  • Network edges are associations; ontology terms overlap.
  • Background, gene length, coverage, and provider mapping can bias enrichment.
  • Historical review snapshot and network graph are not independently re-audited here.

Next steps

  1. Reconcile gating code, saved tables, and narrative counts.
  2. Resolve allele phase and variant validation through approved clinical/research channels.
  3. Check cellular chromosome-segregation mechanism with appropriate experimental assays.
  4. Rerun enrichment with a defensible callable-gene universe and documented mappings.
Conclusion: convergent research hypotheses, no diagnosis or intervention. Sources: [R3–R6].16 / 17
